## 0. The one-paragraph version

In one day the project went from a vision document to a deterministic Rust simulator of an LLM inference
fleet that reproduces **nine named dynamics** with numbers, a dashboard that shows those dynamics three
ways (recorded, live, and stepped through as a walkthrough), an arena that scores policies and can write
new ones, and a public deployment that scales to zero. **11 of 11 selected dynamics have a showcase
walkthrough, 10 run live on the dashboard, and 10 open as a live run from the showcase**; the last step
(walkthrough `set` steps applying to a live run) landed at 20:59 (U59b). Two agent-architecture restarts
took the fleet from 8 merged units an hour to 32.

## 5i. Why a fleet at 2% of capacity reads 55% GPU utilization (2026-09-10 10:58 PDT)

Issao ran 50 replicas at 10 rps and saw utilization 53% under every policy. Reproduced:
`gpu_utilization_mean 0.549`. The metric measures *time inside a step*, which is what nvidia-smi's
GPU-Util measures: every decode step reads the whole model's weights from HBM to produce one token
per sequence in the batch, and that read costs the same for one sequence as for 256. A replica holding a
single sequence therefore steps back to back and is "busy" whenever it holds anything; at 0.2 rps per
replica with ~300-token outputs at ~10 ms a step, that is about half the time. Any routing policy gives
the same number because the arithmetic is per replica.

Decision (Issao): keep that metric as it is, because it is what a GPU counter shows, and add
**useful work over the maximum possible** (`METRIC_GPU_USEFUL_FRACTION`, 71): per step the compute-
priced part in full plus the bandwidth-priced part scaled by batch fill. The same run reads **useful
3.2%**; the live dashboard at 50 replicas / 10 rps reads 55% busy, 0.7% useful. The ratio of the two is
the mean batch fill, which is the sense of batch size Issao asked for.

What it means for autoscaling: GPU-Util saturates at batch 1, so a fleet at 3% of capacity looks half
busy and a fleet at 100% looks barely busier; scaling on it adds replicas that make batches emptier and
leaves the signal unchanged. Scale on queue depth, tokens per second against rated capacity, or the
useful fraction, never on busy time (finding 5's one-line note, now measured).
